## <span class="headings">Current Project: </span> 
### <span class="subheadings">Dark Matter Trigger of Type Ia Supernovae (UCSC | Bowdoin College)</span>

Primordial Black Holes (PBH) are a possible alternative candidate for the dark matter constituents of the universe. I am interested in whether the transit of PBH through white dwarfs can heat temperatures of a critical spatial scale long enough to trigger a runaway thermonuclear reaction before expansion and diffusive processes overcome the perturbation. I conducted numerical-relativistic calculations of a transiting PBH and preliminary results show substantial heating around the black hole. I will build on these calculations with multidimensional hydrodynamic simulations using the hydrodynamic code, FLASH, at UCSC. This will allow us to determine the boundary of when a transit will trigger a Type Ia supernovae. This work is already a senior thesis accepted to the Bowdoin College Library and a manuscript for publication is in development.

## <span class="headings">Past Projects:</span>

### <span class="subheadings">Opacity Calculation Under Average Atom Density Functional Theory (Los Alamos National Laboratory)</span>

Between graduating from Bowdoin College and starting my graduate studies at UCSC, I received a fellowship from Los Alamos National Laboratory (LANL) to work on a project calculating the opacity of different elements. At LANL, I wrote a novel algorithm to calculate opacity under the average atom density functional theory (DFT). This method is more sensitive to plasmon signatures and allows for more accurate modeling of collective electron excitations for time-dependent DFT. This approach has significant impacts for light-curve astrophysics, producing emission lines that were poorly resolved before. The manuscript for this project is in development. 

### <span class="subheadings">Constraining the Nuclear Equation of State from GWs (Bowdoin)</span>

Because we cannot create densities near or higher than nuclear density on Earth, neutron stars (NS) are great "laboratories" for studying nuclear processes and constraining the nuclear equation of state (EOS). Previous studies found that NS are able to capture PBHs. These PBHs will orbit within the NS and precess as they orbit. The gravitational waves (GWs) emitted by such precessing orbits will vary based on orbit periods, modular frequencies, and mass of PBH. We determined that if gravitational waves from such an event are captured, we will be able to further constrain the nuclear EOS as well as determine the mass of the NS which is difficult under normal observations. See my first author publication in Physical Review D: <a href="https://journals.aps.org/prd/abstract/10.1103/PhysRevD.110.064066" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Constraining the Nuclear Equation of State</a>

### <span class="subheadings">Disentangling Abundance Characteristics of Solar Flare (Center for Astrophysics, Harvard)</span>

During the summer of 2023, I participated in the Solar-REU at the Center for Astrophysics (CFA) working with Dr. Katherine Reeves and Dr. Xiaoyan Xie to construct spatially distributed thermal diagnostics on a class M1 solar flare using data taken by X-Ray Telescope (XRT) aboard the solar satellite Hinode. I led the development for creating volume emission measure and temperature maps of the flare in three different time stamps based on spectroscopy data using a novel Python package, XRTpy. We resolved that the “anomalous” coronal abundance of low first ionization potential elements around the flare peak indicates a different solar flare heating mechanism than the general chromospheric evaporation process. This demonstrates that our understanding of the physical mechanisms driving solar flares is still incomplete. See the co-authored paper published in the Astrophysical Journal: <a href="https://iopscience.iop.org/article/10.3847/1538-4357/ae1615" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Disentangling Abundance Characteristics of Solar Flare</a>
